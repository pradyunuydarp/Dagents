\subsection{F1. Responsible-AI practice}
\label{sec:survey-rai}

\paragraph{Principles agree on values and say little about application.}
Jobin et al.\ found five principles that recur across 84 guideline documents,
and wide disagreement on how to apply them~\citep{jobin2019global}. Mittelstadt
argues that principles alone cannot guarantee ethical AI, because they lack
enforcement and shared professional duties~\citep{mittelstadt2019principles};
Hagendorff makes a similar point~\citep{hagendorff2020ethics}. Morley et al.\
catalogue tools that translate principles into practice~\citep{morley2020what},
and later report how far that translation has come~\citep{morley2023operationalising}.
Studies inside companies find launch pressure, metrics that ethics work cannot
meet, and personal risk for the people who raise
issues~\citep{rakova2021where,ali2023walking}, and developers who value
guidelines more than they follow them~\citep{ximenes2026bridging}.

\paragraph{Most approaches decide through people, at fixed points.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Design time}: Value Sensitive Design~\citep{friedman1996value,friedman2008value},
        Privacy by Design~\citep{cavoukian2010privacy}, Ethics by
        Design~\citep{dignum2018ethics}, privacy engineering and Hoepman's eight
        privacy design strategies~\citep{spiekermann2009engineering,hoepman2014privacy},
        and data protection by design and by default under Article 25 of the
        GDPR~\citep{eu2016gdpr,bygrave2017data}.
  \item \emph{Documentation}: model cards~\citep{mitchell2019model}, datasheets
        for datasets~\citep{gebru2021datasheets}, FactSheets~\citep{arnold2019factsheets}
        and data statements~\citep{bender2018data} record decisions after they
        are made.
  \item \emph{Audits and assessments}: internal audit frameworks such as
        SMACTR~\citep{raji2020closing}, external audits~\citep{raji2019actionable,birhane2024ai},
        algorithmic impact assessments~\citep{metcalf2021algorithmic} and
        privacy impact assessments~\citep{wright2012state} are human reviews
        before release or after the fact.
  \item \emph{Toolkits}: AIF360, Fairlearn and the What-If
        Tool~\citep{bellamy2019ai,weerts2023fairlearn,wexler2019what} compute
        metrics and offer mitigations during development; a data scientist
        decides which to apply~\citep{holstein2019improving,deng2022exploring}.
  \item \emph{Governance platforms}: Microsoft's Responsible AI
        Standard~\citep{microsoft2022responsible}, IBM
        watsonx.governance~\citep{ibm2026watsonxgov}, Credo
        AI~\citep{credoai2026platform}, Holistic AI~\citep{holisticai2026enforce}
        and the NIST AI Risk Management Framework~\citep{tabassi2023nist}
        organise these into inventories, risk tiers, evidence and approvals.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Per-request enforcement appears only in runtime guardrails.} Most
guardrails sit at the input and output of a large language model: NeMo
Guardrails~\citep{rebedea2023nemo}, Llama Guard~\citep{inan2023llama},
ShieldGemma~\citep{zeng2024shieldgemma}, Granite Guardian~\citep{padhi2024granite}
and Guardrails AI~\citep{guardrailsai2026onfail}. Dong et al.\ define a
guardrail as an algorithm that decides ``if and how some enforcement actions can
be taken''~\citep{dong2024building}, and Hu et al.\ make a guardrail's
strictness depend on a user trust score, as GRAILS does~\citep{hu2024trust}.
Newer work enforces at the tool calls of LLM agents: CaMeL checks Python policy
functions against capabilities attached to values~\citep{debenedetti2026defeating},
and AgentSpec checks rules in a small language~\citep{wang2026agentspec}.
GRAILS is the one surveyed design that enforces per request on structured data.

\paragraph{Failure behaviour is rarely stated.} Guardrails AI's default action
on a failed check is \code{noop}: the output passes and the failure is
logged~\citep{guardrailsai2026onfail}. CaMeL blocks a call that breaks a policy,
and suggests asking the user in production. Documentation and governance
products record rather than block. Lu et al.'s catalogue of responsible-AI
patterns lists a ``federated learner'' and an ``ethical black box'' as separate
patterns~\citep{lu2024responsible}.

\paragraph{Relation to Dagents.} Dagents shares GRAILS's strategy table, the
deterministic allow-or-deny with a reason of CaMeL and AgentSpec, and the
append-only evidence that FactSheets and the ethical black box propose. Six of
Hoepman's strategies (minimise, separate, aggregate, hide, enforce and
demonstrate) map onto its boundaries. The family is stronger at everything a
runtime cannot do: eliciting stakeholder values, measuring and mitigating
unfairness, documenting models and datasets, mapping controls to regulations,
and independent review. Language-model guardrails also handle free text, which
a rule table over typed fields cannot. As of 16 November 2026 we found no work
that cites GRAILS; Crossref reports no citing works, and we could not query
Google Scholar.
