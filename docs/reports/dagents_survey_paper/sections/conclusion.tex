\section{Conclusion and future work}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We asked one question of \SurveyWorks{} works and industry systems: where do
the decisions about what a request may see, what may leave a site, who may take
part and what may be released get made, and where are they enforced? Each
family we surveyed answers part of it well. Policy engines make fast,
analysable permit-or-deny decisions. Privacy-enhancing technologies provide
mechanisms with proofs. Federated learning frameworks run the training
protocol across sites. Trusted research environments and research networks
control what leaves a site. Regulations and release practices say what evidence
is needed. Few approaches decide the form of the data, enforce it at each
source, cover training, sending and release with the same rules, fail closed,
and leave a record that names the decision maker.

GRAILS showed that ethical restrictions can be decided outside the application
and enforced by a guard. Dagents applies that split to a study that runs across
several organisations. It makes every governance and federation decision with a
total, typed function in a separate process, enforces the decision at four
boundaries at each source and at the coordinator, denies when there is no
decision, and records each decision with the identity of the planner that made
it. In one study on 1,014 real patient records from three hospitals, the Guard
cost 0.0005 AUC, and the release gates rejected a candidate that was better
than the model in use but not by the margin the consortium required.

Future work follows from the limits in \cref{sec:limitations}:
\begin{itemize}
  \item a privacy accountant and a tracked budget for model updates, so that
        clipping and noise can support a differential privacy claim;
  \item signed round manifests and audit records, kept in append-only storage;
  \item a test that the strategy table is monotone in sensitivity, and a
        machine-checked statement of the table's properties;
  \item other ethical dimensions that GRAILS names, such as fairness, as
        planner rules rather than only as release gates;
  \item a study with an independent team, measuring adoption effort and the
        planner's latency at scale against an assembly of existing parts.
\end{itemize}
