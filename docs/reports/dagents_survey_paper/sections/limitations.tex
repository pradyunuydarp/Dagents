\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\subsection{Limits of the design and the implementation}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{A strategy is not a privacy proof.} The planner chooses a
        transformation from a table; it does not prove a bound on what an
        attacker can learn. Generalisation and minimum cohorts do not prevent
        every attack on released statistics (\cref{sec:survey-pets}). Clipping
        and seeded noise bound each model update, but Dagents has no privacy
        accountant and tracks no privacy budget, so it does not provide
        differential privacy.
  \item \textbf{Seeded noise is predictable to the coordinator.} The Guard seeds
        its noise from the request id, the field and the position, so that an
        auditor can reproduce it. For an outgoing model update the request id is
        built from the round id and the site id, which the coordinator knows. A
        coordinator can therefore compute the noise and remove it, so the
        \emph{add noise} strategy protects nothing against the coordinator. The
        stroke study is not affected, because its updates are clipped, not
        noised. A fix would add a secret held by the site to the seed and
        disclose it only to an auditor.
  \item \textbf{Decisions are made one request at a time.} The minimum cohort
        is checked on an exact count, and nothing tracks what earlier answers
        revealed. Combining permitted aggregates can rebuild records
        (\cref{sec:survey-fa}); budgets and difference checks across queries
        are not implemented.
  \item \textbf{The table encodes a judgement.} The strategies in
        \cref{tab:rules}, the trust thresholds (0.4 and 0.75) and the KYU
        weights (0.7 for attributes, 0.3 for history) are choices, as are the
        constants for generalisation, noise and clipping. Typing makes the table
        complete and testable. Whether each choice suits a domain is for a
        consortium to review, and Dagents gives no method for that review.
  \item \textbf{Trust depends on its inputs.} The KYU score is computed from
        attributes the caller passes in, marked as verified or not. Dagents does
        not verify identities or affiliations.
  \item \textbf{Approval names no approver.} The GMA's approval route takes the
        model version and a token with the administrator scope. It does not
        record who approved, or check that the approver is not the person who
        asked for the release. There is also no route for a request that needs
        review by a person, no staged deployment and no monitoring after
        release (\cref{sec:survey-reg}).
  \item \textbf{Evidence is tamper-evident, not tamper-proof.} The audit chain
        detects an edited or deleted record. It is not an append-only store with
        retention rules and signatures, and round digests are content hashes,
        not signatures.
  \item \textbf{Coordination is centralised.} One GMA coordinates a study.
        Recovery after a restart assumes one GMA process with one worker, and
        messaging between agents is in memory or over HTTP; a message broker is
        future work. Live validation on Kubernetes has not been done.
  \item \textbf{Isolation in the published demo rests on code paths.} The
        hosted demo connects to its database with one credential that can read
        every schema. Isolation there holds because every query switches to a
        hospital's role, not because the login is restricted. A real deployment
        gives each site's LMA its own login and database.
  \item \textbf{Architecture is not compliance.} Regulations such as HIPAA also
        require administrative and physical safeguards, proof that controls
        work in practice, and independent audits. No codebase provides those.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Limits of the evidence}

\begin{itemize}
  \item The case study is one study, one model and one dataset, run by the
        authors. Its records are from 1991 to 1996, and the candidate is
        trained and measured on the same hospitals. The cost of 0.0005 AUC in
        \cref{sec:cost} does not generalise to other data or models.
  \item We have not measured the planner's latency at scale, the cost of
        running one LMA per source, or the effort an application team spends
        to adopt Dagents compared with assembling the parts itself.
  \item No independent team has deployed Dagents. The comparison in
        \cref{sec:comparison} is an analysis of designs, not a controlled
        study.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Limits of the survey}

The survey is a structured narrative review by the authors of one of the
systems it compares. Its families and dimensions come from the questions this
paper asks, and another lens, such as cost or usability, would group the same
work differently. Products change quickly; we describe each as its
documentation stood on the date we read it.
