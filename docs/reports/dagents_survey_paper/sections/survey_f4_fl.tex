\subsection{F4. Federated learning}
\label{sec:survey-fl}

\paragraph{Keeping raw data local is not enough.} Federated learning moves
training to the data. Clients send ``focused updates intended for immediate
aggregation'' and a coordinator combines them, across millions of phones
(cross-device) or across a few organisations
(cross-silo)~\citep{mcmahan2017communication,kairouz2021advances,li2020federated,huang2022crosssilo}.
Updates and models leak training data: gradients can be inverted into
inputs~\citep{zhu2019deep,geiping2020inverting}, and membership can be
inferred~\citep{shokri2017membership,nasr2019comprehensive}. One participant can
implant a backdoor~\citep{bagdasaryan2020backdoor}, and Byzantine-robust
aggregation can be broken~\citep{fang2020local}.

\paragraph{Defences are mechanisms inside the protocol.} Secure aggregation
hides each client's update from the server~\citep{bonawitz2017practical,bell2020secure};
user-level and distributed differential privacy bound what a model reveals about
one participant~\citep{mcmahan2018learning,kairouz2021distributed}; norm
clipping limits backdoors~\citep{sun2019really}; and robust aggregators limit
poisoning~\citep{blanchard2017machine,yin2018byzantine}. The model engineer
selects these in code, through a Flower strategy, a TensorFlow Federated
aggregator or a FLARE filter~\citep{beutel2020flower,google2026tff,roth2023nvidia}.

\paragraph{Governance lives outside the learning code, in five places.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{Provisioning and roles}: NVIDIA FLARE's startup kits and an
        authorisation file for each organisation~\citep{nvidia2023flareauthz},
        OpenFL's certificates and plan~\citep{foley2022openfl}, Flower's node
        registration~\citep{flower2026supernodeauth}.
  \item \emph{Site policy files}: FLARE's site privacy file forces filters on
        every job at a site~\citep{nvidia2026flaresitepolicy,nvidia2026flareprivacy};
        Apheris's signed asset policies name allowed models and privacy
        controls~\citep{apheris2026assetpolicies}.
  \item \emph{People}: PySyft's data owner approves each
        request~\citep{ziller2021pysyft}; Fed-BioMed runs a training plan only
        if a node approved its hash, in an optional mode~\citep{cremonesi2025fedbiomed};
        MedPerf supervises federated evaluation~\citep{karargyris2023federated}.
  \item \emph{Ledgers}: Substra's per-asset permissions, under which a trained
        model inherits the intersection of its inputs'
        permissions~\citep{galtier2019substra}, and Swarm Learning's
        blockchain~\citep{warnatherresthal2021swarm}.
  \item \emph{A policy language}: FLA3 evaluates XACML policies when a node joins,
        when it joins a study and in every round before aggregation, fails
        closed, and signs its audit records~\citep{zhang2026building}.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Model release is the least covered stage.} Release rules are
organisational. Google states that ``all future launches of Gboard neural
network LMs will require DP guarantees''~\citep{xu2023federated}, and the FeTS
study, with data from 71 sites on six continents, released its consensus
model~\citep{pati2022federated}. Clinical studies
such as EXAM~\citep{dayan2021federated} and MELLODDY~\citep{heyndrickx2024melloddy}
show federated learning at hospital and pharmaceutical scale, with governance
handled by agreements and committees around the
software~\citep{sheller2020federated,rieke2020future}. Substra is the one
framework where release follows from coded rules. Baracaldo et al.\ propose
FactSheets for accountable federated studies~\citep{baracaldo2022towards}.

\paragraph{Relation to Dagents.} FLA3 is the closest prior art: it decides
participation per round outside the learning code, fails closed and logs every
decision. So gating participation outside the learning code is not new with
Dagents. Less common is the combination of a per-field protection plan,
participation, quorum, release gates and model-version status in one typed
planner, with the planner's hash in each audit record. The family is stronger
in mechanisms and scale: cryptographic secure aggregation (Dagents's
\code{secure\_aggregation} profile only raises the participant floor and
requires a clip bound), calibrated privacy accounting, robust aggregation,
signed records, code-hash approval, hardware attestation, and deployments
across dozens of sites. Dagents's design leaves the learning protocol to such a
runtime (P2).
