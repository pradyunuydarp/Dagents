\subsection{F7. Release governance and regulation}
\label{sec:survey-reg}

\paragraph{Release in machine-learning practice.} Machine-learning systems carry
hidden technical debt~\citep{sculley2015hidden}, and the ML Test Score lists the
tests a production system should pass~\citep{breck2017ml}. TFX validates a
candidate against a baseline before it is pushed~\citep{baylor2017tfx}, and
studies of practice describe the steps teams take to deploy a
model~\citep{amershi2019software,paleyes2022challenges,kreuzberger2023machine,shankar2024we}.
Model registries such as MLflow, SageMaker and Vertex AI store a version's stage
or approval status~\citep{zaharia2018accelerating,mlflow2026registry,aws2026sagemakerapproval,google2026vertexalias}.
The registry stores the state; a pipeline step the team writes, or a person who
changes a status field, decides whether a transition is allowed.

\paragraph{Controlled change in banking and medicine.} Banks add independent
model validation~\citep{fed2011sr117,pra2023ss123}. For medical devices,
regulators approve in advance an envelope of allowed changes: the FDA's Good
Machine Learning Practice principles~\citep{fda2021gmlp}, its final guidance on
Predetermined Change Control Plans~\citep{fda2024pccp,fda2023pccpprinciples},
which rests on section 515C of the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic
Act~\citep{usc2022pccp}, and Article 43(4) of the EU AI
Act~\citep{eu2024aiact}. A change inside the envelope needs no new submission.
This is the closest regulatory analogue to a release gate written as a function.
AEGIS turns these plans into four fixed outcomes (approve, conditional approval,
clinical review, reject)~\citep{afdideh2026aegis}. Feng et al.\ show that
repeated approval of small changes can let accuracy drift
down~\citep{feng2021approval}.

\paragraph{What the law asks for.} \Cref{tab:obligations} lists obligations
that bear on the questions in \cref{sec:introduction}, and whether a program can
check each one per request or per release. Privacy law states principles as
standards of reasonableness: purpose limitation and data minimisation in the
GDPR~\citep{eu2016gdpr} and in India's Digital Personal Data Protection
Act~\citep{india2023dpdp,india2025dpdprules}, and the minimum necessary standard
of HIPAA~\citep{ecfr2026part164,hhs2026minimum}. HIPAA shows the split: for
routine disclosures it allows standard protocols, a field set decided in advance
that a program can apply; for other requests it requires review of each
request by a person. The European Health Data Space issues permits that name the
purpose and the data, and lets only non-personal data leave a secure processing
environment~\citep{eu2025ehds}. The AI Act requires automatic logs (Article 12),
testing against ``prior defined metrics and probabilistic thresholds''
(Article 9(8)) and human oversight (Article 14)~\citep{eu2024aiact}; an
amending regulation adopted in 2026 moved the dates of its high-risk
obligations~\citep{eu2026omnibus}. The NIST AI Risk Management Framework and
ISO/IEC 42001 organise these duties for an
organisation~\citep{tabassi2023nist,iso2023iec42001}.

\paragraph{What stays with people.} Formal work on HIPAA and the GDPR shows that
part of each law can be checked mechanically from logs and process models, while
predicates about purpose, belief or necessity stay with a
person~\citep{deyoung2010experiences,garg2011policy,basin2018purpose}. A program
cannot check that a stated purpose is the real one, that a new purpose is
compatible with the old, that thresholds are clinically right, or that human
oversight works; studies of oversight find it
unreliable~\citep{green2022flaws,laux2024institutionalised,sterz2024quest}. A
2026 study of twelve AI Act compliance checkers found that they ``may foster a
false sense of compliance''~\citep{tao2026empirical}. No regulation says how a
per-request decision is computed, how its logic is versioned, or how to show
that the code that decided is the code that was reviewed.

\paragraph{Relation to Dagents.} Dagents's \code{before\_read} plan supports
minimum necessary and data minimisation; its minimum cohort and
\code{before\_send} support de-identified output; its release gates, missing-metric
rule and planner-decided status changes support testing against fixed
thresholds and controlled change; its audit records, which name the planner's
hash, support automatic logs and traceable logic; and failing closed supports
fail-safe behaviour. The family points to gaps. Dagents has no route for
non-routine requests that need review by a person, no shadow or canary
deployment, no monitoring after release, no control of accuracy drift across a
sequence of releases, and its approval route records no named approver
(\cref{sec:limitations}).
