Analytics and machine learning across data that several organisations hold, and
may not pool, must decide many times a day what a request may see and in what
form, what may leave a site, who may take part in a study, and whether a new
model may be released. We survey \SurveyWorks{} research works, products and
regulations in seven families: responsible-AI practice, policy engines,
privacy-enhancing technologies, federated learning, systems that bring code to
the data, architectural precedents, and release governance. For each, we ask
where the decision is made, what it decides, where it is enforced, and what
evidence it leaves. We start from GRAILS, which separates ethical restrictions
from application code with a planner and a guard. We find that most families
govern one part of a multi-source study well, and that few decide the form of
the data outside the store that holds it, enforce at each independent source,
apply one rule set to reading, training, sending and release, fail closed, and
record which logic decided. We then state the design of Dagents, an open-source
framework that extends GRAILS to such studies, as ten principles tied to code:
every decision is a total function over algebraic data types in a separate
process, enforced at four boundaries, denied when no decision is available, and
recorded with the identity of the planner. In a study on 1,014 real patient
records from three hospitals in the International Stroke Trial, the guard cost
0.0005 AUC, and the release gates rejected a better model that missed the
required margin. We state the limits of the design and of this survey.
